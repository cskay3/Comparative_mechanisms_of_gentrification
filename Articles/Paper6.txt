5.Discussion
Thisresearchnotonlyidentified arobustandexpanding bodyof
literature ongentrification inprovincial citiesbutalsoexamined the
factors,actorsandtypesofthisprocess.Basedontheliteraturereview,it
isevidentthatgentrification inprovincial citiesisgenerallydelayed,has
alowerintensity, andisoftenincomplete compared togentrification in
metropolises (e.g.,Bridge,2003;Dutton,2005amongothers).Tallon
andBromley(2004),Bromleyetal.(2007),andProuseetal.(2015)

videdevidencethatthepositionofcitiesbypopulation sizemattersin

vincialBristoldisplayonlysomeofthesecharacteristics. Butexamples

smallercities,suchasKingston, Canada,whereadominant university
playsacentralrole(Meligrana &Skaburskis, 2005).Whencomparing
themechanisms ofmetropolitan andprovincial gentrification, Lees
(2000),Dutton(2003),SmithandGraves(2005),andButler(2007)
foundthatprovincial gentrification hasmanysimilarities withmetro-
politangentrification, buttheprocessalsohasitsownpeculiarities.
Dutton(2005)arguedthatmetropolitan andprovincial gentrification
aretwodifferentprocesses.
Theeconomic benefitsofgentrification knownfrommetropolises
canalsobeobservedinprovincial cities –thearrivalofrelatively rich
gentrifiers bringsmoreincometoprovincial citiesfromtaxesdueto
higherpropertyvaluesandbusinesstaxes,andprovincial gentrification

2006;Ciesi ´ołka&Ma´ckiewicz, 2020;Dutton,2005;Torres-Out ´on,
2020).Compared tothemetropolises, however,theseeconomic benefits
aresomewhat lower.According tostatepricemaps,rentpricesininner
citydistrictsofCzechprovincial citiesareabout59%compared toinner
Prague.Thisislessbeneficial tohomeowners anddevelopers inpro-
vincialgentrification, butbeneficial toprovincial marginal, studentand
transitory gentrifiers. However, theeconomic costsassociated with
providing housingforpeopledisplaced bygentrification andtheneed
forgreatersupportforneighborhoods affectedbythearrivalofdis-
placedpeople,whichalsoexistinprovincial cities,cannotbeignored
(Glynn,2008;Prouseetal.,2015).
According toSlater(2002),gentrification “trickles ”or“cascades ”
downfrommetropolitan toprovincial citiesthroughthehierarchical
spatialdiffusion. Atkinson andBridge(2005)notedthesubsequent
transferofcapitalanddevelopers' effortstorenovateinnercitiesfrom

theintroduction ofarapidtramlinktothelargernearbycityof
Waterloo.

6.Conclusions
Thestudyofgentrification hasoverwhelmingly focusedonlarge
urbancentersintheliterature, leavingprovincial citiescomparatively
unstudied inthisregard.Thisreviewaimedtoscrutinize themost
important differences inthemechanisms, residential actorsandtypesof
gentrification betweenmetropolises andprovincial citieswithless
diverseeconomies andlesspolarizedpopulations. Acertainlimitation of
thisstudyisthatthecasestudiesanalyzedareonlyfromtheGlobal
Northandtheirfocusispredominantly onthedemandsideofthe
process.
Basedonthereviewedliterature, wecanconcludethatgentrification
inprovincial citiestendstobesomewhat weakeranddelayedcompared
tothatinmetropolises. Provincial gentrifiers placemoreemphasis on
thepracticalbenefitsofinner-city livingoverculturalandentertainment
amenities andlifestylemotivations, whicharemoresignificant for
metropolitan yuppiesandyupps.Theliterature suggeststhatgentrifi-
cationinprovincial citiesoftenexhibitsamixedpatternwithinneigh-
borhoods, involving pioneer,marginal, ytus,student,andtraditional
gentrifiers, sometimes itisinfluenced bylocalgovernment. Thethreatof
displacement duetoupwardeconomic pressurehasoftenbeenlessse-
vereinprovincial cities,andsomevulnerable residents remainin
gentrified neighborhoods. Intheprestigious inner-city neighborhoods of
metropolises, gentrification tendstobemorepronounced anduniform
initseffects;ithasamoreextensive coverage, resultingingreater
displacement oforiginalresidents.
Whilethereviewed papersoftensuggestthatgentrification inpro-
vincialcitiesisgenerallylessintensethaninmetropolises (thefirstinput
assumption) andlessintenseinsmallerprovincial citiesthaninmid-
sized,thischaracterization doesnotapplyacrossallprovincial cities
andtypesofgentrification. Extensive studentgentrification canbefound
insmallerprovincial university cities,strongpioneering gentrification is
oftendescribed forsmallerprovincial citieswithvaluablehistorical
cores,androbusttourismgentrification cantakeplaceinsmallpro-
vincialcitieswithsignificant touristattractions. Smallerprovincial citiesalsohavegoodpreconditions fornew-build andgreengentrification.
Theliterature analysisconfirmed thatclassicgentrification occursona
smallerscaleinthesecities,withmarginalgentrification beingmore
widespread inprovincial citiescompared tometropolises (thesecond
assumption).
Existingstudiesonprovincial gentrification arebasedondiverse
methodologies thatprovideempirical evidencefromprovincial cities
worldwide. However, onlyafewstudieshavedeveloped solidtheoret-
icalframeworks thatprovidenewconceptual ideas.Futureresearch
should,therefore, paymoreattentionnotonlytothedifferentcharac-
